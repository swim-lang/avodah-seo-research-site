Google profiles and office locations

Separate websites do not automatically qualify for separate Google Business Profiles. First confirm the actual business or practitioner arrangement and existing profile ownership. We have not verified a pending sister-site application, so we cannot yet describe this as Google delaying approval.

A mailbox or virtual office is not eligible. A coworking office needs Avodah staff, clear signage and client access during business hours. A genuine service-area business may hide its address if it travels to customers; this is not a workaround for an ineligible office.

An existing eligible location may be usable now, depending on the business arrangement. If the proposed new offices are needed to qualify, wait for those facts. For a genuine move, update the existing eligible profile rather than create a replacement; Google requires verification again.

How five articles come from one focused conversation

Before each practice interview, we bring five distinct questions tied to the approved service priorities. We ask for practical experience and reasoning, not a lecture or a legal memo. We only produce an article when the interview and verified sources support a useful answer.

This is an editorial mix, not five versions of the same article. No duplicated pages across sites, invented attorney opinions, or location pages without real service evidence.

Low-friction review, not a lawyer in every production meeting

  1. 1
    Agree on the boundaries once

    Approve audiences, services, tone, topics to avoid, authorship and reviewers. We maintain a reusable editorial guide and a record of approved positions.

  2. 2
    Record the real perspective

    With participant consent, interview each practice lead using a prepared question queue. No identifiable client matters. If an answer needs research or nuance, flag it rather than inventing it.

  3. 3
    We research and prepare complete drafts

    Use the interview as the source for the attorney's views. Check supporting facts against primary sources. Separate experience and opinion from statements about the law; include sources and highlight claims that need legal confirmation.

  4. 4
    One reviewer, one consolidated batch

    Deliver five drafts per practice with a short review sheet showing the key claims and open questions. The attorney approves, edits or holds each article. One consolidated revision round is included. Proposed turnaround: five business days, to be confirmed after the first batch.

  5. 5
    Publish only what is approved

    Written approval remains mandatory. Missed reviews pause publication. We handle formatting, titles, links, author/reviewer credits, dates and quality checks. Changes to approved legal substance go back for review.

The efficiency comes from capturing expertise upfront and concentrating review into batches. It does not remove attorney review. New legal developments, deadlines, remedies and jurisdiction-specific advice receive deeper review or are deferred.

Why this approach

Google recommends helpful, original content with clear expertise and sourcing. Publishing more low-value pages is not a strategy. Our proposed approach pairs Avodah's actual experience with focused questions and a documented review process.
